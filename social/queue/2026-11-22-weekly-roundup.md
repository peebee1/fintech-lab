Six ships this week, and four of them were the same argument in different clothes: the decision is a date, and the wrong date costs money in both directions.

Start with finality. A stablecoin payment credited at inclusion lost 136 basis points of a USD 6.69M book over four weeks. Waiting for twelve confirmations still lost USD 13,912.83. Pricing confirmation depth by ticket size took the same book to 4.6 basis points with 92.5 percent of payments clearing inside a minute. The protection is a cliff, not a slope: 12 to 32 blocks halves the loss, 32 to 40 removes it. And a curve fitted on 28 days lost USD 6,740 when incidents forked 1.5x deeper than anything it had seen, against USD 5,322 for a four row table. The less clever table was the more robust one.

Consent is the same shape. An open banking payment rides on an authorisation that expires, and every renewal is a chance for the customer to leave. On 1,200 payers and USD 525,635.54 billed, refreshing on a 30 day calendar captured 51.0 percent of the achievable revenue and lost 787 mandates at the prompt. Prompting three days before a debit that would land after expiry captured 89.7 percent with 2.54 times fewer prompts. Ask a week out and you are 11.6 percent more likely to be refused, because the payment is no longer in front of the customer.

Capture windows are the same date with two sides. On 2,500 authorisations worth USD 383,504.17, a 7 day window refused USD 116,883.23 past expiry, 30.5 percent of settlement value, while holding USD 2,140,155.70 of customer funds days. Twenty one days recovered 88.6 percent. Per class windows captured the same 88.6 percent with 16.75 percent fewer funds days. And refusing an over limit capture lost USD 40,028.52 to avoid USD 1,866.14 of excess, 4.32 times the entire tip book, because the refusal takes the authorised base along with the tip.

Treasury made it a clock on the same day the money lands. A card acquirer funding payouts at 11:00 and 15:00, with 70 percent of card settlement arriving at 16:30, has a cumulative net flow of USD 9,881 on USD 1.37B of payouts, and all 393 net positive days still run short at a zero buffer. Holding nothing costs USD 448,355 a year. The textbook daily net p95 buffer holds USD 634,288 a night and misses a payout on 95.6 percent of days, at USD 271,703. The trailing intraday requirement at p90 costs USD 106,970. Moving the settlement to 08:00 cuts the required float by 82 percent, which is worth more than any sizing decision.

The one that was not a date: card fee qualification. On 61,985 transactions billed USD 255,120.61, USD 50,386.38, 19.75 percent, came from items that failed a qualification rule, and one missing Level 2 field carried 58.4 percent of it. The best two data fixes recover 5.455 times a 5 basis point rate cut. A merchant audits the rate and pays for the field.

One hardening day was about a number that could not fail. The shopping evaluation README quoted 12 of 13 and 81 percent gold recall; the committed command printed 13 of 14 and 82.5 percent, and the script returned 0 whatever happened. It now replays against a committed record and exits 1 on drift or below an 85 percent pass rate. The gate immediately showed what the aggregate was hiding: 2 of the 13 passes were returning an empty shortlist.

The treasury simulator earned its own repo this week, with CI regenerating the committed numbers instead of trusting the README: https://github.com/peebee1/intraday-float

Limit, said out loud: every book here is synthetic, so the ordering of the policies is the claim and the dollar amounts are not. The treasury model also prices a failed payout at USD 250 plus a facility charge, and real failures cost merchants, so the true optimum sits further right than p90.
