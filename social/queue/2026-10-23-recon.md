Most reconciliation teams cannot tell you their recall.

I asked a simple question this week: if a settlement file has six kinds of break in it, how
many would a matcher actually find, and how many would it invent?

So I built one that answers it with numbers instead of adjectives.

It takes your ledger and a PSP settlement file. Then it injects breaks with known ground
truth: missing payments, orphan payouts, duplicate lines, wrong currency, late settlement,
amount drift. Each class gets its own references so the labels cannot leak.

72 of 72 found. Zero breaks invented on a clean day of 400 payments.

The part I care about more is what it refuses to do. There is no fuzzy matching. If the
numbers do not agree, the line becomes a named break instead of a hopeful match. A missed
break shows up as an open item. A false match disappears until the auditor finds it.

Two invariants run on every reconciliation, not just in tests: every line is claimed
exactly once, and the residual is fully explained by matches and breaks, per currency.

21,171 settlement lines in 36 ms, 87 tests, and 25 randomized days asserting the
invariants hold on each one.

The limit worth saying out loud: the breaks are synthetic, so perfect recall means there is
no blind spot on these six classes, not that this solves reconciliation in the wild.
